How I’d connect the sales pipeline to revenue and delivery capacity

A retainer business grows only as fast as it adds clients, keeps them, and staffs them. Move the inputs and watch revenue, margin and hiring needs change together. The inputs are illustrative, not Belkins data.

Pods to add

Each month: clients = clients × (1 − churn) + new clients. Revenue = clients × retainer. Pods = clients ÷ clients per pod, rounded up.

What I’d look at first

Five questions I’d bring to the Belkins numbers

These come from how retainer agencies make money, not from inside knowledge. I’d test each one against your data in the first month.

Which clients actually make money?

A per-client P&L: retainer, pod cost and tools, plus the cost of each meeting that was actually held, not just booked.

When do clients leave?

A monthly revenue bridge (new, expansion, lost) and retention grouped by signing month, watched closely at the 3- and 6-month renewal points.

Is the forecast tied to capacity?

A 12-month rolling forecast that links the sales pipeline to revenue, headcount and hiring lead time, so a strong sales month doesn’t overload delivery.

How much does currency move margin?

Revenue in dollars and payroll in local currencies means FX can hide in the variance. I’d report it as its own line.

How fast does cash come in?

An invoicing calendar and receivables tracker to shorten days sales outstanding. I cut DSO by 15% this way at DNS Hospitals.
